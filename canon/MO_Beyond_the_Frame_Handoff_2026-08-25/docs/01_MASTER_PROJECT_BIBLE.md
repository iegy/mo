# MO — Beyond the Frame
## Master Project Bible V1

## 1. Core concept

MO is a bilingual interactive entertainment website presented as a colored cinematic comic/manga. It should feel less like “reading a webpage” and more like entering a story that physically responds to the reader's scroll.

The defining interaction is:

**Scroll → Mo moves → camera/panel changes → the story happens.**

The website is not intended to become a heavy game. It lives between a webcomic, animation, interactive picture book, and light game-like experience.

## 2. Main protagonist — Mo

Mo is a small lovable robot with a large screen head, cream/sage mechanical body, segmented arms, orange-tipped antenna, and wheel/tread base. His personality is curious, kind, smart, sometimes stubborn, easily stressed, and physically expressive.

Mo never speaks normal dialogue. His silence is a core strength of the character and makes him globally readable. He communicates through expressions on his dark screen, posture, antenna, hands, movement speed, timing, and soft electronic sounds.

Comedy comes from Mo's reactions and timing rather than verbal jokes.

## 3. Story tone

Priority order:
1. Comedy
2. Adventure
3. Mystery
4. Emotion
5. Action
6. Science fiction

The stories are accessible to children but should contain visual and emotional layers for teenagers and adults.

## 4. World model

The series is not confined to “the world of code.” Code/programming is part of Mo's origin and visual language, but the story can travel to radically different worlds.

Examples:
- a city above the clouds;
- a train that travels between impossible worlds;
- a city frozen in time;
- a mechanical desert;
- a pencil-drawn world;
- a world under water;
- a human city;
- a place with no color;
- a blank page.

### Frames
Most worlds exist inside boundaries called **Frames**. Most inhabitants cannot perceive those boundaries.

### The Margin / الهامش
The Margin is the strange in-between space connecting stories/worlds. It may contain orphaned doors, windows with no walls, disconnected stairs, scraps of roads, fragments of abandoned panels, or pieces of unfinished stories.

### The Blank / الفراغ
The Blank is a long-term mysterious threat. It appears to erase worlds, but its true purpose is more complicated. In early seasons the audience should assume it is destructive, then gradually suspect it may be containing something worse.

Visually, The Blank is absence rather than a monster.

## 5. Larger mystery

Mo possesses abilities that should not be normal:
- sees Frames;
- crosses Frames;
- repairs pieces of worlds;
- eventually creates new pieces of reality.

Mo's origin is deliberately withheld. His world may not be his true home. It may have been built to keep him isolated from something.

PIX knows something about this history.

## 6. PIX

PIX is a tiny living fragment of orange light/energy, not a robot. PIX flies, changes shape slightly, and communicates through movement, glow, simple visual symbols, and digital chirps.

Core dynamic:
- Mo thinks; PIX acts.
- Mo tries to understand; PIX rushes ahead.
- Mo becomes braver over time; PIX becomes more cautious.

PIX is playful and mischievous but becomes visibly frightened around The Blank.

## 7. Human characters

Humans may appear occasionally. The universe should remain open to humans, robots, strange creatures, and world-specific beings.

## 8. Chapter design philosophy

Every chapter must work as a complete adventure for a first-time reader.

Each chapter should contain:
- clear beginning;
- local problem;
- exploration/adventure;
- climax;
- resolution;
- one or more small clues about the larger mystery.

Each chapter releases complete rather than split into Part 1 / Part 2.

## 9. Reader relationship

At first, Mo does not know he is in a website or comic-like world. Occasionally he may look toward the screen because he senses something strange. Over time, the story may develop a meta-layer where Mo suspects an unseen “observer.” This should evolve slowly rather than becoming a gag immediately.

## 10. Hidden discovery

Each chapter should contain roughly 3–5 optional Easter eggs. They are not coins, scores, or required game objectives. They are rewards for curiosity:
- jokes;
- tiny animations;
- lore clues;
- future chapter references;
- creator signature “MH”;
- recurring symbols.

## 11. Long-term scalability

The project should be built around a reusable **MO Story Engine**, not eight unrelated hardcoded pages. New chapters should become new content packages that reuse the same motion/scene system.
